why you should read this book

Brooklyn is throughout her childhood the reluctant hero, going from a mildly impoverished lifestyle in Los Angeles to internet stardom. She struggles with the idea that the version of herself that everyone on the internet likes to see is not who she really is. The book explores her transformation into not only a sort of anonymity but into having more agency in her own fate.

Brooklyn stands in contrast to El Hermano Mayor, a gargantuan and well-read bandit who she runs into by chance in the desert. They become fascinated by each other and try to understand the ways in which the other's brain doesn't work properly. But the person who truly challenges her is Rachel, a woman who is everything she is not -- naturally easygoing and friendly, yet defenseless -- and the two find themselves inexplicably drawn to each other.

Big Bend has one of the darkest skies in the United States. Blood of Agave is a book I wrote about a few people who I imagine may have lived under those stars, and how that place changed them. In a world where nothing ever happens, this is perhaps an exercise in magical realism -- but that's why we read books.
